## Who this guide is for

You are looking at a flat in Durrës or on its coast — Golem, Plazh, Shkëmbi i Kavajës, Qerret, Mali i Robit or the Bay of Lalëz — and you do not live in Albania. Most buyers here come from Kosovo, the Czech Republic, Poland, Hungary, Germany and Switzerland (often the Albanian diaspora), Italy and Ukraine, with a budget of roughly €50,000–150,000 and a plan to combine summer use with letting. You want to know what things cost, what the purchase adds on top of the price, which papers to bring, and what to check before any money moves. That is what the next pages cover, in that order.

## Durrës in one page

Durrës is Albania's main port (about 80% of the country's foreign trade goes through it), a university town of some 150,000 people, and the beach most of Tirana drives to. Its property market is the cheapest coastal entry in the country and the one with the broadest foreign demand — Century 21 put foreigners at about half of coastal buyers in 2026 (monitor.al). Two things shape it: the November 2019 earthquake (M6.4, epicentre near the city), which left a lasting premium on new construction and a discount on old stock in Plazh and Shkëmbi i Kavajës; and the Tirana–Durrës–airport railway, promised for 2027 after repeated delays, which would put the city 22 minutes from the capital.

**City centre, Vollga and Currila.** The old town, the seafront promenade and the hill above it. New towers near the port ask more than €2,500/m² (monitor.al, April 2026); the research base's working range for the centre is €900–1,500/m² and for Vollga €1,000–1,800/m². Demand is local families and Tirana commuters, so it lets year-round. **Plazh (Iliria, Hekurudha, Pelikan).** The city's mass beach strip. Most of the stock is more than 30 years old; monitor.al quotes €1,200–1,700/m² in 2026, and €2,000/m² applies to roughly 5% of new first-line units. Gross short-let yields of 7–11% (Investropa) are the best on the coast, but the season is short. **Golem.** The long pine-backed beach with the most Kosovar and diaspora owners. Second and third lines ask €1,100–1,500/m², the first line €2,000–2,500/m² with no new construction (monitor.al). ## What buying costs on top of the price

Apartment sales in Albania are VAT-exempt, so a new build carries no extra 20%. The buyer's own costs are small; what varies is who ends up paying the 2% transfer tax and whether you hire a lawyer.

## The purchase, step by step

Foreigners buy flats, houses and commercial property on the same terms as Albanian citizens, including inheritance; only agricultural land is excluded (an Albanian company or a lease of up to 99 years are the usual routes). No residence permit, tax number or local bank account is needed for the purchase itself — you can wire the price from abroad to the notary's escrow account.

1. **Reservation.** A deposit ("kaparë") of 5–10%, up to 30% on new builds, deducted from the price. Get the refund terms in writing; a reservation that is not refundable if the build stops has cost buyers €20,000 before.
2. **Due diligence, 1–2 weeks.** The property card ("kartela e pasurisë"), the encumbrance extract, the cadastral map ("harta treguese"), 30+ years of title history, the building permit ("leje ndërtimi") and the certificate of use ("leje përdorimi"). A lawyer is not required by law and is the only real protection — there is no title insurance in Albania, and a first-instance court case takes about a year, an appeal several.
3. **Preliminary contract.** For a finished home, a notarised pre-contract. For a new build, only a notarised purchase-order contract ("kontratë porosie") registered with ASHK protects you from the same unit being sold twice; a private contract is void for transfers.
4. **The notarial deed, one day.** Ownership passes only by notarial deed. The price goes through the notary's escrow account (the notary cannot handle cash above 150,000 lek); the notary withholds the taxes and, if you do not speak Albanian, a sworn translator reads the deed and co-signs it.
5. **Registration with ASHK.** Filed by the notary, no visit needed; officially 21 days, in practice 2–4 weeks, months for a complicated title. You then receive the new property card in your name.

With clean papers the whole cycle takes 4–8 weeks. A mortgage from an Albanian bank is close to unavailable to a non-resident without local income; developers' interest-free instalment plans until handover are the common alternative on new builds.

### Documents to bring

- Passport (the number is recorded at ASHK).
- A sworn translator if you do not understand Albanian — arranged by the notary, paid by you.
- A notarised power of attorney only if someone signs for you; issued abroad, it needs an apostille (Albania joined the Apostille Convention in 2004) and an Albanian translation.
- A marriage or civil-status certificate: the deed states your marital property regime.
- Proof of the bank transfer of the price; the notary may ask for proof of funds under anti-money-laundering rules.
- An Albanian tax number is on many agencies' lists but not required for individuals by the official sources found; you will need one later, through a representative, to declare rental income.

### Checks that stop the usual failures

- **Is it registered at all?** About a tenth of the country's territory is unregistered; ask for the kartela before anything else.
- **Legalised self-build ("leje legalizimi").** The title is real, but compensation to the former landowner may be unpaid — a ground for challenge.
- **The developer's mortgage.** A bank charge on the plot passes to the units: ask for the encumbrance extract of the parent plot, not just the flat.
- **Extra floors and changed layouts.** Since ASHK Instruction 1557/2024 a mismatch between the built plan and the permit means registration is refused; compare the flat with the approved plan before paying.
- **Utility debts follow the meter.** Certificates from OSHEE (electricity), the water utility and the building administrator.
- **Year of construction and seismic standard**, especially in Plazh and Shkëmbi i Kavajës, where buildings fell in 2019.
- **The "local way".** Under-declaring the price in the deed saves the seller tax and leaves you unable to prove what you paid; one contract, one bank transfer.

## What owning costs per year

- **Property tax.** Currently 0.05% a year of the fiscal value (the state reference price per m² times the area), for residential property. Durrës still applies a single city-wide reference from 2016 — sources give 67,500 or 71,200 lek/m² (monitor.al; Homezone) — so a 60 m² flat pays roughly 2,000–2,150 lek a year, about €22–23. A reform to 0.1–0.2% of market value, with a 50% first-home relief and higher rates for flats let short-term, was in consultation in mid-2026 and would apply from 2029; it is not enacted.
- **Electricity.** 10.2 lek/kWh incl. VAT up to 700 kWh a month, 11.4 on the whole month above that (ERE Decision 322, valid for 2026). The average household uses about 230 kWh a month, some 2,350 lek (≈ €25); summer air-conditioning and winter heating are what push a beach flat over that.
- **Water.** Durrës has the most expensive water in the country: 70 lek/m³ water plus 50 lek/m³ sewerage plus a 200 lek monthly fee in the city and Plazh; the Kavajë unit (Golem, Qerret, Mali i Robit) charges 50 + 30 + 200. The regulator's benchmark bill for 5 m³ is 800 lek in Durrës. Supply runs about 13 hours a day, which is why every building has a roof tank and a pump.
- **Building fee.** 1,000–1,500 lek a month in a post-1993 building with a lift, up to 3,000 in premium blocks; old blocks 400–500 (Law 55/2025 makes the administration fee mandatory). Resort complexes with pools and security publish nothing reliable — expect several times more.
- **Waste.** Durrës has not published its 2026 tariff; by analogy with Vlorë and Sarandë, 3,000–4,000 lek a year.
- **Insurance.** From 5,000 lek a year (≈ €54), roughly €1–1.5 per m²; banks require it with a mortgage, and after 2019 it is worth having anyway.
- **Internet.** About 1,690 lek a month. The heating season runs from mid-November to mid-March.
- **If you let.** Individuals pay a flat 15% on rental income, declared annually in DIVA by 31 March; short lets through platforms are taxed on the amount after the platform's commission, with no other deductions, and need no business registration. Non-residents pay the same and get a credit at home under one of Albania's roughly 46 double-tax treaties. On resale, capital gains are taxed at 15%.

## Questions to ask the seller

1. Can I see the current property card (kartela) and the encumbrance extract — for the flat and for the plot?
2. Does the building have a building permit and a certificate of use, and does the built layout match the approved plan?
3. What year was it built, and to which seismic standard? What was repaired after November 2019?
4. How did you acquire the title — purchase, legalisation, inheritance — and is any restitution claim known?
5. Are there unpaid electricity, water or building-fee bills?
6. What is the monthly building fee, who administers the building, and is there a pool or security to pay for?
7. Will the price in the deed be the full price I pay?
8. Is the price with or without VAT, and who pays the 2% transfer tax?
9. For a new build: will the purchase-order contract be notarised and registered at ASHK, what is the payment schedule, what happens if handover is late, and is the plot mortgaged?
10. How many hours a day does the water run, and is there a tank and pump? How far is the sea, and (in Golem) which sewer line serves the building?
